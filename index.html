Colors come in pairs

Every side of the cube has exactly one opposite. Knowing these pairs is how you check a corner is truly home — not just "showing white."

W
Y

White ↔ Yellow

Always opposite each other.

G
B

Green ↔ Blue

Always opposite each other.

R
O

Red ↔ Orange

Always opposite each other.

So once white is solved: Front is one fixed color, and Back is always its opposite — never a different color from the other two pairs.

Three buttons, six moves

A 2×2 cube only needs three sides to solve: Right, Top and Front. The little ' mark means "turn that same side backward." A little 2 (like U2) means "turn that side twice" — a half turn instead of a quarter turn.

R

Right side

Turns the side facing your right hand.

U

Top side

Turns the side facing the sky.

F

Front side

Turns the side facing you.

1

Build the white face

Every little corner piece on the cube has 3 colors on it. We're going to gather the 4 corners that have white and build them into a matching white face — like a tiny puzzle.

Step A — Find a white corner.

Look at the cube. Find any corner piece that has a white sticker on it. It will also have 2 other colors, like white-green-red.

Step B — Check its neighbors.

Those 2 other colors tell you exactly where the piece belongs — right where the green side and the red side meet. That's its "home."

Step C — Turn one side at a time, and watch.

Turn R, U, or F — just one turn — and look at your white corner. Did it get closer to its spot? Great, keep going. Didn't help? Turn that same side back (that's the ' move) and try a different side instead. There's no trick to memorize here — just turn, look, and adjust. Every white corner can be walked into place this way.

⚠️ Heads up: some fumbling is normal here

Here's the honest truth: U turns the entire top layer at once — so once you have a white corner placed, any U turn will carry it to a different spot (it'll still show white, just not lined up with its neighbors anymore). There's no trick that fully protects already-placed corners while you work on the rest — that's just how this stage is.

What actually helps: don't panic if a placed corner gets bumped — just re-place it again. Focus on getting 2-3 corners "roughly right" before worrying about perfection, then use careful single turns (checking after each one) for the last couple. It's normal for this part to take real trial and error, even for experienced cubers.

Want a shortcut? (optional)

Once a white corner is sitting in the top layer, this move can help walk it down faster. It won't always be the right move to use, so don't worry if it doesn't help right away — the one-turn-at-a-time method above always works too.

R U R' U'
Step D — Repeat for all 4.

Find the next white corner and do the same thing — turn one side at a time and watch it get closer. Keep going until all 4 white corners sit together, with matching colors on the sides. There's no fixed number of turns here — just try, watch, and adjust. That's normal, even for grown-up cubers!

Line up the top
